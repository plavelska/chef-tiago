Skills, kitchens, concepts, culture

Skills, not packages.

Useful before the first plate leaves the pass: reading a menu, a team, a supplier problem, tired service, or a wild idea and finding the part that needs heat.

Core skills

What goes into a project.

Menus are built from the reality of the kitchen, not from fantasy boards: season, suppliers, prep pressure, margin, service pace, and the story a guest should understand without needing a lecture.

The result is food with personality that can still survive repetition: recipes with clear hands, exact steps, sensible costs, and enough memory to become part of the place.

A strong concept is not just a name, a mood, or a nice plate. It is the logic that connects the menu, the table, the staff, the price point, the music, and the kind of night people are supposed to have.

The work turns loose energy into a usable food language: bold enough to be noticed, simple enough to execute, and specific enough to belong to one place only.

This is the practical layer that decides whether the idea works on a busy night: stations, mise en place, handovers, storage, cleaning routines, timings, and the invisible friction that slows a team down.

He brings order without making the kitchen stiff: clearer prep, cleaner communication, better flow, and a pass that can carry pressure without losing appetite.

A restaurant can have the right menu and still feel wrong if the team is tense, confused, or tired in the wrong places. The human side of service matters as much as the food.

He helps teams understand what matters, how to talk during pressure, where standards need to be exact, and where confidence can replace panic.

Behind the instinct is a very practical operator's brain: budgets, ordering, waste, staffing pressure, supplier reliability, and the difference between a beautiful idea and a sustainable one.

He is useful when a project needs taste and discipline at the same time: sharper decisions, fewer leaks, and a clearer sense of what deserves energy now.

The small signals matter: when a table wants comfort, when the night wants volume, when a dish needs less explanation, when music changes the appetite, and when the best move is simply generosity.

That instinct helps a project feel alive rather than decorated. The food has to carry flavor, but the whole experience should carry memory.

Selected projects

Where the skills got tested.

2025-2026

The new challenge: fusion with a pulse.

Portuguese instinct, Ibiza appetite, world flavours.

Mira Lounge Ibiza is the new challenge: a place for fusion cuisine through Portuguese instinct, Ibiza appetite, world flavours, and a service rhythm that wants more than a polite dinner.

His role is concept and kitchen leadership: shaping the food direction, building rhythm with the team, and making sure the menu, the team, and the atmosphere feel like one idea.

2019-2020 + 2021-2022

A long love story with serious roots.

Simple food, exact hands, real family.

Bottega il Buco is not just a line on a CV. He was part of the Ibiza story around the New York il Buco family: local producers, Italian staples, bread, focaccia, crudos, pastas, market plates, desserts, and a place with soul.

The role moved through the real stuff: keeping food simple but exact, protecting quality, understanding the team, and returning to the Bottega family when the food needed his hands again. The recent collaboration with Stevie Benanty has helped push the place into a more creative chapter.

2023-2026

Spanish coastline, Goan cliff, real setup work.

Beauty made operational.

Milagros, known publicly as Milagro Goa, is a Small/Ozran Vagator restaurant with direct beach access, sunset views, cocktails, and a coastal Spanish spirit. In Goa, he supported its setup while working with chef Silvia Grimaldo.

The place had beauty already: a cliffside mood, a menu remembered for dishes like lobster spaghetti and calamari salad, and Silvia's elegant Spanish hand. The role was to help make that beauty operational: kitchen structure, prep logic, service flow, team rhythm, and the practical systems that let a restaurant feel relaxed without becoming loose.

2021

Technique, soul, and flavour that refused to sit still.

An eclectic world needed a food language.

At WOW Ibiza, the role was head chef. The brief in his CV is exact: worldwide food, techniques from Michelin-star kitchens, the comfort of soul food, and flavour combinations with real appetite.

That made the work bigger than running a pass. WOW sat between San Rafael and Santa Gertrudis as a restaurant, cocktail bar, garden hangout, concept-store world, and social place with music in the air. The job was to give that eclectic energy a food language: local produce, global ingredients, brasserie confidence, clean plating, steady workflow, and dishes bold enough to belong to the night.
